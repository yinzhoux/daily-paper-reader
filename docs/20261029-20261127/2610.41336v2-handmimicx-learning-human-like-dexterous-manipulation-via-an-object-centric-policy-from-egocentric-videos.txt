The framework consists of three stages. First, we reconstruct the demonstrated hand–object interaction from the input video, recovering the hand–object motion trajectory, object geometry, and task-relevant interaction priors. Abstract — Learning dexterous manipulation from human videos remains an open challenge, particularly in the context of transferring a single demonstration into a spatially generaliz-able robotic skill and maintaining physically plausible human-like interactions. Unlike prior works that emphasize hand-motion imitation, we introduce an object-centric framework for acquiring dexterous skills from egocentric human RGB-D videos. Within our framework, we formulate the reinforcement learning (RL) objective in terms of object motion to decouple the underlying skill from the specific hand trajectory and impose constraints on hand–object interaction states to pro-mote more anatomically natural hand configurations. Extensive experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate that our method enables efficient learning from human videos and generalizable skill transfer across novel spatial configurations. I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in embodied intelligence have demon-strated the potential of learning robot manipulation policies from human videos [1], [2], [3], [4]. However, directly transferring human demonstrations to robots remains chal-lenging due to the gap between human motion and robot embodiments, as well as the discrepancy between visual observations and physical execution [5], [6], [7]. Reinforcement learning (RL) provides a powerful mech-anism to bridge this gap by optimizing interaction behav-iors under physical constraints in simulation. Under this paradigm, human demonstrations can be transformed into physically feasible robot manipulation policies. However, acquiring a robust and generalizable skill from a single human demonstration remains challenging. Existing RL approaches largely rely on hand motion as the primary supervision signal, learning residual actions to refine the arXiv:2610.41336v2 [cs.RO] 9 Nov 2026 reference motion [5], [8], [9], [10]. While this paradigm enables precise motion imitation, it relies heavily on accu-rately reconstructed hand–object trajectories, making perfor-mance sensitive to even minor misalignments and favoring kinematic reproduction over the acquisition of transferable skills. By contrast, object-centric policies [11], [12] prioritize desired object states, shifting the learning objective from replicating a demonstration to capturing what the task is meant to accomplish. Nevertheless, their disproportionate emphasis on object-level task success often leads to unrealis-tic hand–object interactions, including physically implausible contacts and unnatural finger configurations. In light of these challenges, we propose HandMimicX , a novel object-centric reinforcement learning framework for acquiring an underlying manipulation skill from a single egocentric human video. The “X” indicates moving beyond trajectory mimicry toward generalizable skill acquisition. To bridge egocentric human videos and dexterous control, we jointly reconstruct hand-object interactions (HOIs). However, camera motion degrades reconstruction accuracy, particularly in terms of hand–object alignment. We therefore refine the reconstructed hand translation using depth to improve spatial consistency. To transcend the trajectory constraints inherited from the human demonstration video, we condense the full-sequence reconstructions into object geometry and interac-tion priors to retain the most critical task-relevant elements. Using the reconstructed geometry, we train an RL policy to manipulate the object to arbitrary 6-DoF target poses, while the interaction prior regularizes the resulting hand–object interactions toward physically plausible configurations. For real-world deployment, we use DAgger [13] to distill the policy and reduce its reliance on privileged information. Ulti-mately, our framework transforms a single human demonstra-tion video into a transferable dexterous skill that generalizes beyond the demonstrated spatial configuration. In summary, our contributions are as follows:  

> •

We propose HandMimicX , a novel object-centric RL framework that learns a generalizable dexterous manip-ulation skill from a single human video.  

> •

We build a joint 4D HOI reconstruction pipeline that recovers spatially consistent hand–object motion from dynamic egocentric views.  

> •

We enhance object-centric RL with an extracted interac-tion prior, regularizing policy learning toward physically plausible and anthropomorphic dexterous manipulation. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Human–Object Interaction Reconstruction 

Recovering human–object interactions (HOIs) from visual observations provides the geometric interface between hu-man videos and downstream robot learning [14]. HOLD [15] leverages a compositional articulated implicit model to jointly reconstruct articulated hands and objects using com-positional SDF and contact constraints. HO-NeRF [16] sep-arately learns shape and appearance prior knowledge of hands and objects with a neural representation and proposes a pose estimation system for hand-object interaction from sparse views. These methods, however, rely on either scene-specific optimization or pre-built hand models, limiting their scalability to in-the-wild videos. Another line of work, exemplified by HaMeR [17], scales up a ViT-based backbone for 3D hand reconstruction, yielding accurate and robust hand mesh recovery, while HaWoR [18] and Dyn-HaMR [19] couple camera tracking with hand-motion estimation for global 4D hand recovery from unconstrained videos. Recent studies [14], [20], [21] have developed more generalizable integrated frameworks for reconstructing overall hand–object motion. Nevertheless, these approaches remain susceptible to cumulative errors, particularly in hand–object alignment, compromising downstream manipulation. 

B. Learning Robot Manipulation from Human Videos 

Exploiting human videos to train robotic models has been actively studied in recent years. Recently, [1], [2], [22], [23] have utilized human video for pretraining robotic manipulation models; for instance, EgoScale [1] uncovers a clear log-linear relationship between data scale and hand-action prediction loss and demonstrates that this loss strongly correlates with real-robot dexterous manipulation perfor-mance. By leveraging large-scale human video corpora [24], [25], [26], [27], [28], these works envision a future in which robots acquire diverse skills from abundant human demonstrations. In parallel, advances in simulation [29], [30] have established real-to-sim-to-real as an alternative paradigm for robot learning from videos [3], [4], [31], [32], in which policies are optimized via RL to reproduce motions demonstrated in human videos. Our proposed HandMimicX ,instead, emphasizes learning a transferable skill via RL from a single human demonstration video, moving beyond mere imitation of the demonstrated trajectories. 

C. Object-Centric Reinforcement Learning 

Unlike residual RL [8], [32], [33], which remains an-chored to a reference trajectory, object-centric RL [34], [35] directly optimizes task-relevant object-state transitions. Dex4D [11] learns a generalizable task-agnostic grasping policy, while X-Sim [36] trains a reinforcement learning (RL) policy in photorealistic simulations reconstructed from RGB-D human videos. To simplify RL for robotic hand control, Human2Sim2Robot [37] combines geometric fabric control with a PCA-based action space, but this formulation can lead to degenerate hand configurations. These prior methods either target grippers or neglect the naturalness and anthropomorphism of hand configurations during grasping. By contrast, we transfer physically grounded hand–object interaction patterns from human videos to promote feasible dexterous manipulation. III. M ETHOD

We em-ploy a correspondence-aware encoder to capture geometric relationships between the current and target object point clouds while preserving pointwise correspondences, yielding a compact and expressive latent representation of their rela-tive pose. The remaining observations are concatenated and passed through an MLP encoder to obtain a proprioceptive embedding. All embeddings are jointly fed into the actor to produce a 26-dimensional action vector. The predicted action is executed in the simulation environment to interact with the object and receive the corresponding reward. Our critic estimates the expected cumulative future return from the current state under the actor policy, providing a value signal to guide policy optimization. Throughout the process, the interaction prior shapes the reward by measuring the deviation from the reference hand pose, encouraging the policy to maintain physically plausible hand configurations. Additional privileged information, including contact and collision signals, is used to assess policy performance.

c) Training Strategy: The high-dimensional action space of the robotic hand makes direct policy optimization challenging. We therefore adopt a two-stage training scheme consisting of pretraining and finetuning. The pretraining stage equips the policy with general manipulation capabil-ities and provides a favorable initialization for downstream learning by training on over 3200 objects [25], [44], as shown in Fig. 4. Each object is associated with hundreds of reference hand poses. We then finetune the policy on each task to acquire the desired skills, guided by the object and hand–object interaction priors extracted from the correspond-ing HOI video. Notably, pretraining and finetuning share the same policy architecture and reward formulation. We pretrain the policy for 30K iterations and finetune for 10K per task across 4,096 parallel environments. SAPG [45] is used to optimize both stages, with its four blocks employing distinct exploration distributions to collect diverse rollouts and broaden coverage of the action space.

C. Real-World Deployment 

As shown in Fig. 7, our system comprises a 6-DoF UR5 robotic arm equipped with a 20-DoF Wuji hand. A single side-mounted Intel RealSense D435 camera provides visual observations for object tracking. We calibrate the camera extrinsics with respect to the robot base frame. During inference, FoundationPose estimates the object’s 6-DoF pose. Points sampled from the object mesh are transformed into the robot base frame using calibrated camera extrinsics to obtain the current object point clouds, synchronized with robot proprioception. The goal point cloud is predefined according to the manipulation objective. The student policy is conditioned on these inputs to predict the next action. Our real-world evaluation examines whether the proposed object-centric policy captures the manipulation skills un-derlying human video demonstrations and generalizes their execution across spatially varying trajectories. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented HandMimicX, an object-centric reinforce-ment learning framework for acquiring a dexterous manipula-tion skill from a single egocentric human demonstration. Our framework integrates depth-refined hand–object reconstruc-tion, correspondence-aware object representations, and inter-action priors that regularize contact formation and finger con-figurations. By coupling object-level goals with demonstrated interaction structure, HandMimicX enables the robot to adapt its manipulation behavior to spatial configurations beyond the original demonstration. Experiments across eight tasks show improved success rates over object-centric baselines, while ablations support the contributions of interaction pri-ors, geometric correspondence, and pretraining. Real-world deployment also demonstrates the feasibility of transferring these learned behaviors to physical manipulation. VI. L IMITATIONS 

Despite its strong performance, our framework has several limitations. It relies on reconstructed object geometry, esti-mated hand poses, and contact predictions, whose errors can propagate into policy learning. Moreover, our contact rep-resentation is limited to discrete region-level states, without modeling finer-grained physical contact forces. Our evalua-tion of the framework across different embodiments and on bimanual manipulation tasks also remains limited in scope. Future work will investigate more robust human–object inter-action perception pipelines, incorporate force-aware contact representations, and extend the framework to more embodi-ments and manipulation tasks.
