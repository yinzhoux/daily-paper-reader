## Abstract 

Learning how actions change visual states is essential for prediction and decision-making in embodied tasks. Existing action-conditioned JEPA world models typically predict fu-ture representations at discrete observation intervals, leav-ing the temporal evolution of action effects implicit. We in-troduce ActField, a continuous-time JEPA world model that explicitly models latent state evolution through an action-conditioned vector field integrated over physical time. Act-Field separates action-independent evolution from action-induced changes, with the latter modulated by the latent context. A reference-conditioned representation and multi-step dynamics loss further support accurate long-horizon prediction. Together, these components provide a struc-tured and temporally continuous formulation for predicting action-conditioned visual dynamics. Experiments across LIBERO, DROID, and BEHAVIOR demonstrate improved visual prediction fidelity and more accurate action-induced motion prediction over existing world models. Further eval-uations on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate the utility of ActField in improving downstream policy planning through VLM-guided action selection. 

## 1. Introduction 

Robotic manipulation [20, 24, 37] involves complex inter-actions between the embodied agent and its surrounding en-vironments. Predicting how these interactions change the environment [9, 10, 14] provides an effective means of as-sessing the downstream consequences of actions before ex-ecution. World models [8, 12, 19, 29] provide a foundation for this capability by learning how the environment evolves. For robotic manipulation, an effective world model must ac-curately capture action-conditioned dynamics, as different action sequences from the same observation can produce distinct robot motions, contacts, and object displacements. Together, these results suggest that leaving action-driven evolution implicit limits the model’s ability to predict action-dependent visual outcomes. This motivates a central question: 

Can explicitly modeling action-driven latent evolution yield more faithful action-conditioned predictions? 

In this paper, we propose ActField , an action-conditioned latent vector field model that predicts visual dynamics by accumulating local action-driven changes in latent space over time. ActField maps pretrained visual fea-tures into a compact dynamic embedding conditioned on a shared reference observation, then evolves it using a ve-locity field that takes the reference embedding, current dy-namic embedding, action, and physical time as inputs. We structure the field as f +Gψ (a), where f captures evolution independent of the action input and G transforms the action embedding ψ(a) according to the current latent context. In-tegrating the field over successive action intervals composes these local effects into future embeddings while allowing the action influence to vary with the evolving context and elapsed time. We jointly supervise the visual feature recon-struction and integrated dynamics to align the learned latent evolution with observed visual changes. As illustrated in Figure 1, the learned latent evolution more faithfully reflects the environment’s underlying physical dynamics, yielding decoded predictions that better match simulator outcomes than Discrete JEPA. For downstream decision-making, Act-Field predicts visual futures for candidate action sequences proposed by a behavior policy, and a vision-language model evaluates these predictions against the task instruction to select a sequence for execution. Experiments across sim-ulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate con-sistent gains in both world-modeling accuracy and down-stream decision-making performance. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We identify implicit action-driven evolution as a key lim-itation of discrete-time action-conditioned JEPA models in faithfully capturing environment dynamics. • We introduce ActField , a structured action-conditioned latent vector field that explicitly models and integrates lo-cal action effects over physical time. • We show consistent gains in world-modeling accuracy and downstream decision-making through experiments across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Action-Conditioned Latent World Models 

Action-conditioned world models predict the consequences of control actions to support planning and policy learn-ing [11, 39], with latent approaches learning compact dy-namics from visual observations [13, 42, 43]. Represen-tative approaches, Dreamer [14–16] learns policies from imagined trajectories generated by a latent world model, whereas TD-MPC [6, 17, 18] uses learned latent dynam-ics to optimize action sequences at inference time through model-predictive control. Joint-Embedding Predictive Ar-chitectures (JEPA) [4, 22, 31] learn predictive represen-tations by forecasting target embeddings rather than re-constructing pixels. DINO-WM [47] learns an action-conditioned predictor over frozen DINOv2 [35] features for latent-space planning. V-JEPA 2-AC [3] scales JEPA pretraining on large-scale video and adapts it to action-conditioned robot modeling, while LeWorldModel [28] sta-bilizes end-to-end pixel learning with a Sketched-Isotropic-Gaussian regularizer. Despite these advances, action-conditioned JEPAs directly predict future representations from current representations and actions, leaving the inter-vening action-driven evolution implicit. ActField instead models this evolution as an action-conditioned latent vector field integrated over physical time to obtain future represen-tations. 

2.2. Continuous-Time Dynamics Modeling 

Continuous-time models describe state evolution through learned differential equations [33, 34, 46]. Neural ODE [5] models continuous state evolution using a neural-network-parameterized differential equation, while Latent ODE [38] embeds this dynamics model into a latent-variable frame-work for modeling irregularly sampled time series. More recently, continuous-time formulations have shown promise for embodied world modeling. TAWM [32] conditions la-tent dynamics on transition duration and trains across mul-tiple temporal resolutions, enabling embodied prediction and control across varying observation frequencies. ODE-World [26] further models visual dynamics as a physical-time latent velocity field and obtains future representa-tions through numerical integration. However, existing continuous-time world models have not explicitly focused on modeling how control actions induce the evolution of vi-sual representations over physical time. ## 4. Methodology 

We introduce ActField , an action-conditioned JEPA world model that represents visual dynamics as a continuous-time latent vector field. As described in Sec. 3, conventional action-conditioned JEPA models directly predict the next timestep visual representation from the current representa-tion and control action. Our ActField instead introduces a compact reference-conditioned latent state and explicitly models how this state evolves under control actions over continuous physical time. Specifically, given a reference observation, a current observation, and an input action se-quence, ActField constructs a reference-conditioned latent state, explicitly models its local evolution with an action-conditioned vector field, and integrates the field over time to obtain future representations. The same dynamics can be recursively integrated over successive control action steps to produce longer-horizon visual predictions. An overview of ActField is shown in Figure 2.

## 5. Experiments 

To evaluate the effectiveness of ActField, we conduct ex-periments across multiple embodied benchmarks as well as real-world robotic tasks. Our evaluation seeks to an-swer the following questions: (1) Does ActField predict future visual observations more accurately than baseline world models over short and long horizons? (Sections 5.2 and 5.4) (2) How accurately does ActField predict action-induced motion? (Sections 5.2, 5.3, and 5.4) (3) Can Act-Field improve a fixed behavior policy’s task success through VLM-guided action selection? (Section 5.2) (4) How do ActField’s architectural components and training objectives contribute to its predictive performance? (Section 5.3) 

5.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate ActField on three benchmarks: LIBERO [25], DROID [21], and BEHAVIOR [23]. We further assess its utility for action selection on two real-world manip-ulation tasks. We compare ActField with representative JEPA-based world models, including DINO-WM [47] and LeWM [28]. Since these methods were originally evalu-ated under different experimental settings, we run all base-lines using their official implementations under a common evaluation protocol. Further implementation and evaluation details are provided in Appendix A.

5.3. Ablation Studies 

Training objectives. We investigate the individual and combined contributions of feature reconstruction and the multi-step dynamics loss to predictive performance. As shown in the right panel of Figure 4, replacing the multi-step dynamics loss with single-step endpoint supervi-sion (Single Step) leads to a reduction in PSNR, indicat-ing that one-step prediction alone is insufficient to main-tain accuracy over recursive rollouts. This highlights the importance of supervising the integrated dynamics across multiple prediction horizons to mitigate error accumulation during long-horizon prediction. Removing the feature re-construction loss also degrades performance, highlighting its role in anchoring predicted dynamic embeddings to the frozen visual representation space. By constraining the inte-grated predictions in the shared visual feature space, this ob-jective helps preserve visual consistency throughout latent evolution. The full model achieves the best performance, demonstrating that multi-step dynamics learning and fea-ture reconstruction provide complementary benefits for ac-curate and visually consistent long-horizon prediction.

Discrete JEPA directly predicts the next latent representation from the current representation and action, while Joint Field parameterizes the complete latent veloc-ity using a single action-conditioned network without the decomposition in Eq. 5. As shown in the left panel of Figure 4, Joint Field achieves higher PSNR than Discrete JEPA, highlighting the advantage of continuous-time latent dynamics over discrete transitions. Reference conditioning. We further examine the role of reference conditioning by removing the reference observa-tion from both the dynamics encoder and decoder. As re-ported in Table 3, removing reference conditioning consis-tently increases LPIPS across LIBERO, DROID, and BE-HAVIOR, with the average LPIPS increasing from 0.1326 to 0.2119. The reference therefore serves as a shared visual anchor for both latent dynamics modeling and feature reconstruc-tion, improving the consistency of predicted representations over long-horizon rollouts. These results highlight the im-portance of reference conditioning in learning accurate and visually consistent latent dynamics across diverse environ-ments. Conclusion 

In this work, we propose ActField , a continuous-time JEPA world model that explicitly models action effects through a latent vector field integrated over physical time. By decom-posing latent dynamics into autonomous and action-induced components, ActField captures how control actions shape latent state evolution. A reference-conditioned represen-tation and multi-step dynamics loss further support accu-rate long-horizon prediction. Experiments across LIBERO, DROID, and BEHAVIOR demonstrate improved visual pre-diction fidelity over existing world models, while physi-cal dynamics probing and ablation studies validate the pro-posed design. Furthermore, evaluations on VLM-guided action selection in simulation and real-world manipulation demonstrate the practical utility of ActField for predictive decision-making in embodied systems.
